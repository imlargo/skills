# Channels

## Slack and Teams

- One complete message, not a greeting followed by the question. The reader should be able to act without asking "what's this about?".
- Reply in the thread. A new top-level message only for a new topic.
- Mention only people who must act or decide, and say what you need from each ("@ana can you confirm the cutoff date?"). `@channel` / `@here` only for something everyone must see now.
- In public channels, prefer them over DMs for work questions: others learn from the answer and someone else may know it.
- Formatting: plain sentences. Line breaks between ideas, a short numbered list for several items, inline code for names of things, links with a label. No headers, no bold for decoration.
- Keep it to a few lines. If it needs more, write the doc and post the link with the one-line summary.
- Edit a message to fix a mistake instead of sending a correction, unless people already acted on it; then reply with what changed.
- Close the loop: when your question is solved, say how in the thread.

## Email

- **Subject:** what it is about and, when there's an ask, the ask: "Approval needed: Q3 hosting budget by Friday", "Release 2.4 moved to June 12". Never "Hi", "Quick question" or "Update".
- **Body:** greeting with the name, the point in the first sentence, the needed context, the ask with its date, a short close. Short paragraphs.
- **Recipients:** `To` is who must act; `Cc` only who must know. Say why you added someone ("Adding Laura, who owns billing"). Move people to `Bcc` when they're no longer needed and say so.
- **Replies** keep the thread and quote only what you're answering. Answer every question asked, in order.
- **Attachments and links:** say what each one is for. Mention them in the text, so a missing attachment is noticed.
- Sign-off: a plain close ("Thanks," / "Best,") and the name. No inspirational signatures.

## Async Updates

- Lead with the state in one line (on track, at risk, blocked), then what changed since the last update, what's next, and what you need from whom.
- Same structure every time, so readers find things without reading everything.
- Report risks as soon as you see them, with a proposal, not at the deadline.

## Choosing the Channel

- A quick, low-stakes question: chat.
- A decision, an approval, anything external or that must be on record: email or the ticket.
- Something long or with several options to compare: a doc, linked from the message.
- Conflict, sensitive feedback, or a thread past three back-and-forths without progress: propose a short call, then write the outcome back in the thread.
