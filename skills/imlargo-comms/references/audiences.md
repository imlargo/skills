# Audiences

## Register

| Reader | Register | What they need |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Teammates | Direct, casual, technical terms fine | The specifics: file, PR, error, decision |
| Manager or lead | Direct, concise | Status, risks, decisions needed from them, options with a recommendation |
| Stakeholders and product | Clear, no jargon | Impact on users or dates, what you need decided, trade-offs in their terms |
| Non-technical people | Plain, friendly | What changes for them and what they should do |
| External contacts | Courteous and efficient, a notch more formal | Context about who you are if new, the ask, an easy way to answer |
| Recruiters | Friendly, brief | Real interest or a clear no, availability, the facts they asked for |

Formality follows the other person: mirror how they write to you, never more casual than they are on first contact.

## Explaining Technical Work

- Start from the effect: "Exports that took 3 minutes now take 10 seconds", not the refactor behind it.
- One analogy at most, and only to something the reader knows. If an explanation needs the mechanism, give the one fact that matters.
- Turn options into consequences: "Option A ships Friday without CSV import; option B ships next Wednesday with it. I recommend A."
- Estimates come with their confidence and what they depend on. Never round uncertainty into a promise.
- Name technical risk by what it could cause (lost data, downtime, a delay), and what you're doing about it.

## Upward

- Bring a recommendation with every problem. "X is blocked. I propose Y; I need your OK by Tuesday."
- Bad news early and in the first line. A late surprise costs more trust than the news itself.
- Don't send the full history. Summarize; offer details on request.

## External

- First contact says who you are and why you're writing them specifically, in one or two lines.
- Make answering easy: a yes/no question, two concrete time slots, or the exact information you need.
- Nothing internal that isn't meant to leave the company: names of other clients, internal tools, blame.
