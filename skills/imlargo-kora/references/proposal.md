# Proposal

Not a quote or a feature list: a study of the problem and an engineering proposal. Besides what will be built, it explains why it's done that way and what the business gains from each decision.

## Writing Rules

- Readable without technical knowledge. Explain each technical term where it appears and collect it in a glossary.
- Complete and transparent: answer every reasonable doubt in writing before the investment decision. The document is the reference for the whole relationship.
- Name the risks instead of hiding them, each with its mitigation.
- Tie every claim to proof: delivered systems still running, references the client can check.
- A short "how to read this document" at the start, and a closing that invites questions before signing.
- Facts, prices and dates come from imlargo or the diagnosis. Mark anything missing instead of filling it in.

## Structure

1. **Context and strategy:** executive summary, the client's business and current situation, problems to solve, objectives (general, specific, explicitly not objectives, and how success is measured), value proposition, the long-term vision with a module roadmap stated as vision, not commitment.
2. **The solution:** scope of the service and of the product, how it relates to the client's current tools (integrate with what works, replace only what limits), out of scope, features of the first phase, user roles, architecture in plain language, technical decisions and why.
3. **How we work:** methodology (analysis first, deploy pipeline from day one, working software in production every week), collaboration and communication (weekly review on the deployed software, one business contact), roles and responsibilities, who decides what, phases, timeline, deliverables per stage, change management.
4. **Engineering and guarantees:** quality practices, testing, scalability and maintainability, information security, infrastructure and deploy with estimated monthly operating cost, technical and user documentation, training, support after delivery.
5. **Ownership, risks and conditions:** intellectual property, the client's autonomy over the solution, data ownership, risks and mitigations, assumptions, constraints and exclusions.
6. **Commercial terms and close:** engagement model and price (see [engagement-models](engagement-models.md)), what's included and what's billed apart, payment, terms, notes, future opportunities, next steps.
7. **Annexes:** glossary, detailed requirements per module.

Size the document to the project: a small engagement keeps the same parts, shorter.
