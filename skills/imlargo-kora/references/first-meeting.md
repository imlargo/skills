# First Meeting

The meeting sells the definition phase, not the project. imlargo talks for about six minutes; the rest belongs to the client.

## Presentation

1. **Who we are:** the site and the work done, briefly.
2. **How we work:** three phases, always in order: define, build, evolve. Each ends with something concrete; the client can stop after any of them.
3. **Define:** the phase to propose first, at a fixed price. For an operating company it means mapping how it works; for an idea it means landing it: who the first version is for, what it does, what waits, and what it costs to get there. It ends with the written scope, the plan by phases with cost and time, and a clear recommendation, even if it's not to build.
4. **Hand over:** "Hasta aquí hablé yo. Ahora quiero escucharte."

## Listening

Topics for a product idea: the user, the user's own client, what's essential in the product, the business model, and what would make it worth it. For an operating company: the current process, where time and money are lost, and what result the business expects (see `imlargo-projects`, kickoff).

- Ask the first question and let the conversation cover the rest; the list is so nothing is missed, not a script to read aloud.
- Write down the client's words, not yours: if they say "alumnos", write "alumnos".
- "Por definir" is a valid answer; the definition phase exists for that.
- The two decisions that weigh most on a product are who it's for first and how it makes money.

## Closing

1. **First-version draft:** sort what came up into "first version" and "later". If everything lands in the first version, ask: "Si solo pudieras lanzar con tres de estas, ¿cuáles serían?"
2. **Summary:** read it back and ask what's missing or wrong. Send it after the meeting together with the proposal.
3. **Next step:** the definition. The proposal states its scope, duration and price; with the plan in hand, the client decides whether to build, with Kora or with whoever they want.
