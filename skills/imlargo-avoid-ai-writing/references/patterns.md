# Patterns

Each entry: the pattern, then the fix. Words cover their inflected forms (`leverage`, `leveraging`, `leveraged`). A word that's the literal, correct term in context stays ("a robust estimator" in statistics, "landscape" in a map app).

## Words

### Always replace

| English | Use instead |
|---------|-------------|
| delve, dive into, deep dive | look at, cover, or say the thing |
| leverage, harness, utilize | use |
| robust, comprehensive, seamless | reliable, complete, smooth, or a measurable claim |
| crucial, pivotal, vital, paramount | important, or why it matters |
| landscape, realm, tapestry, ecosystem (metaphor) | the field, the actual parts |
| testament to, underscores, highlights the importance of | shows |
| foster, empower, elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge | what concretely changes |
| navigate (the complexities of) | handle, deal with |
| streamline, optimize (vague) | what got faster or shorter, by how much |
| cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class, game-changer, revolutionary | the specific capability |
| meticulous, intricate, nuanced, multifaceted | careful, detailed, complex, or show it |
| embark, journey (metaphor) | start, the work |
| ever-evolving, fast-paced, dynamic (filler) | cut |
| in order to, due to the fact that, at this point in time | to, because, now |

| Español | Mejor |
|---------|-------|
| sumergirse en, adentrarse en, profundizar en (relleno) | ver, revisar, o decirlo directo |
| aprovechar al máximo, potenciar, impulsar | usar, mejorar, o qué cambia |
| robusto, integral, sin fisuras, de clase mundial | confiable, completo, o un dato |
| crucial, fundamental, clave (repetido) | importante, o por qué importa |
| panorama, ecosistema (metáfora) | el campo, las partes concretas |
| es un testimonio de, pone de manifiesto, subraya | muestra |
| revolucionario, disruptivo, de vanguardia, innovador | qué hace exactamente |
| llevar al siguiente nivel, marcar un antes y un después | qué cambió y cuánto |
| en el mundo actual, en la era digital, hoy más que nunca | cortar |
| cabe destacar, vale la pena mencionar, es importante señalar | decirlo |

### Flag in clusters

Fine alone, a tell when two or more share a paragraph: `significant`, `key`, `essential`, `effective`, `enhance`, `ensure`, `various`, `numerous`, `valuable`, `insights`, `framework`, `approach`, `solution`, `impactful`, `notably`, `ultimately`. Spanish: `significativo`, `esencial`, `eficaz`, `garantizar`, `diversos`, `valioso`, `enfoque`, `solución`, `sin duda`, `en definitiva`.

## Phrases

### Openers

- "Great question!", "Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "I'd be happy to help", "¡Claro!", "¡Excelente pregunta!" → cut; start with the answer.
- "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era where", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "En un mundo cada vez más..." → cut; start with the claim.
- "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "Let's break it down", "Vamos a sumergirnos" → cut.
- "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "The truth is", "Spoiler:", "La realidad es que", "Te cuento" → state the point.
- "Imagine a world where..." and rhetorical-question openers ("Ever wondered why...?", "¿Alguna vez te has preguntado...?") → state the fact.
- Recap-flattery: "You raise an excellent point about X" → answer X.

### Closers

- "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Feel free to reach out", "Espero que te sirva", "No dudes en escribirme" → cut, or one concrete next step.
- Recaps: "In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall", "En resumen", "En conclusión" before repeating what was just said → cut the recap.
- Generic future: "The future looks bright", "Only time will tell", "This is just the beginning", "El futuro es prometedor" → cut, or a concrete next step.
- Engagement bait: "What do you think?", "Agree?", "Drop a comment", "¿Y tú qué opinas?" → cut, or a specific invitation.
- Social endorsement: "This is worth your time", "Thank me later", "Bookmark this" → say why it's useful.

### Transitions

`Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`, `That said`, `It's worth noting that`, `Importantly`, `Además` (at every paragraph), `Por otro lado` (with no contrast), `Asimismo`, `Cabe resaltar` → cut, or use a plain connector the logic actually needs ("so", "but", "because").

### Hedges and intensifiers

- Stacked hedges: "could potentially", "may possibly", "might perhaps", "podría eventualmente" → one hedge, only if the uncertainty is real.
- Hollow intensifiers: `genuinely`, `truly`, `really`, `actually` (for emphasis), `incredibly`, `realmente`, `verdaderamente`, `sumamente` → cut.
- "Real" and "actual" inflation: "real impact", "actual results" → cut the adjective, or give the result.
- Narrated candor: "To be honest", "I'll be transparent", "In the interest of full disclosure" → just say it. (A personal voice skill may allow one sparingly.)

### Vague authority

"Experts agree", "Studies show", "Many believe", "It is widely recognized", "Según los expertos" → name the source, or cut the claim.

## Structures

- **Reveal contrast:** "It's not X, it's Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y.", "No es X, es Y", and the countdown ("It's not the price. It's not the features. It's the trust.") → state Y directly. One per piece at most, only when the contrast is the argument.
- **Rule of three:** triads of adjectives or clauses as padding ("fast, reliable, and scalable") → the one or two that are true and specific, or a number.
- **Manufactured punchlines:** a short dramatic sentence after a paragraph ("And that changes everything.", "Simple as that.", "Y eso lo cambia todo.") → cut.
- **Aphorisms:** "Code is read more than it's written", quotable one-liners closing a section → cut unless it's the actual point and isn't a cliché.
- **Colon into a triple:** "Three things matter: speed, clarity, and trust." → the content, in a sentence.
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as", "stands as", "acts as", "boasts", "features", "funciona como", "se erige como" for "is" or "has" → "is", "has".
- **False agency:** "The data tells us", "The market decided", "The code wants" → name who did what.
- **Superficial -ing analysis:** "...highlighting the importance of X, showcasing Y" tacked onto a sentence → cut, or a separate sentence with the actual point.
- **Significance inflation:** "a pivotal moment", "marks a significant milestone", "un hito" for a routine release → say what shipped.
- **Synonym cycling:** "the tool... the platform... the solution... the system" for one thing → one name throughout.
- **Negation chains and tailing negations:** "No setup, no config, no hassle." as copy, "..., no guessing." → a real clause, or cut.
- **Same-opener runs:** three sentences starting with "This" or "It" → vary by restructuring.
- **Prefab humility arcs:** "I used to think X. Then I learned Y.", "Aprendí que...", "Me tomó años entender..." → the lesson as a plain claim with its evidence.
- **Stock reactions:** "I was blown away", "This one stuck with me", "The line I keep coming back to" → what specifically, and why.

## Formatting

- **Em dashes:** zero in prose, in English and Spanish. Use a comma, period, colon or parentheses. Double hyphens count too.
- **Bold:** at most one phrase per section, for something the reader must not miss. Never bold as decoration or as inline headers ("**Speed:** it's fast").
- **Bullets:** only for content that's really a list (steps, options, parameters). Reasoning goes in sentences. Never lists of bare noun phrases ("Improved performance / Better UX / Enhanced security").
- **Headings:** none on a short answer. Sentence case, not Title Case. No emojis in headings.
- **Emojis and hashtags:** none, unless the channel and author use them (then sparing, at line ends).
- **Excessive structure:** headings, bullets and a summary for a three-paragraph answer → paragraphs.
- **Leaks:** unfilled placeholders (`[Your Name]`, `{company}`), citation artifacts (`【1】`, `turn0search`), `utm_source=chatgpt.com` in links → remove or fill.

## Rhythm and Content

- **Uniform length:** every sentence 15-20 words, every paragraph three sentences → mix short and long sentences, let paragraph length follow the content.
- **Shuffle test:** if paragraphs could be reordered without the reader noticing, the text has no argument. Connect them, or cut what doesn't build on what came before.
- **Low density:** a paragraph that restates the previous one in new words → cut it.
- **Generic test:** a sentence that could appear unchanged in another company's post or another project's docs says nothing about this one. Replace it with the specific fact, or cut it.
- **Patch or rewrite:** if more than half the sentences have tells, rewrite from the facts instead of patching phrase by phrase.

## Examples

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| "Great question! In today's fast-paced development landscape, leveraging robust caching is crucial." | "Cache the product list: it's read on every page and changes once a day." |
| "This isn't just a refactor. It's a fundamental shift in how we think about state." | "The refactor moves all writes into one reducer, so the screen and storage can't disagree." |
| "Our new dashboard is fast, intuitive, and powerful." | "The dashboard loads in under a second on 50k rows and keeps your filters in the URL." |
| "I hope this helps! Let me know if you have any other questions." | (cut) |
| "En el mundo actual, es fundamental aprovechar al máximo las herramientas de IA para potenciar la productividad." | "Uso agentes para escribir código, y leo todo lo que generan antes de hacer merge." |
| "No es solo una librería, es una nueva forma de construir." | "Es una librería de componentes para Svelte con accesibilidad probada en cada componente." |
| "The platform serves as a comprehensive solution, showcasing our commitment to quality." | "The platform handles billing, invoices and tax reports in one place." |
